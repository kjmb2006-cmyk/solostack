# How to Build a Client Portal Without Hiring a Developer

If you've ever emailed a client a PDF, then re-sent it three days later because they "couldn't find it," then watched that same file land in a fourth email thread with a slightly different file name — you already know why a client portal matters. It's not about looking fancy. It's about not losing a week of your life to "can you resend that?"

The problem is that "client portal" sounds like a custom-built web app with a login screen, which sounds like a $5,000 invoice from a developer you don't have. It isn't, anymore. You can put together something that feels dedicated, branded, and professional using tools built for exactly this, and most of them are priced for a one-person shop. This guide walks through three realistic paths, what they cost right now, and what you give up at each price point.

## What a solo consultant's client portal actually needs to do

Before picking a tool, it helps to be honest about the job. For a 1-5 person consulting practice, a client portal almost never needs to be a custom application. It needs to:

Give each client one link they can bookmark and come back to. Show them the status of their project, deliverables, and invoices without them emailing you to ask. Let you upload a file once instead of attaching it to six different threads. Keep each client's view separate from every other client's view, so nobody sees someone else's contract by accident.

That's it. Everything past that — custom workflows, client-facing dashboards with charts, e-signature chains — is a nice-to-have, not a requirement for most solo practices in year one.

## The three realistic paths

| Approach | Example tool | Starting price | Best for | Setup time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Repurpose a tool you already pay for | Notion | $10/user/month (Plus, billed annually) | Consultants who already live in Notion and have 1-10 active clients | 1-2 hours |
| Dedicated client-portal/CRM platform | SuiteDash, Dubsado, HoneyBook | $19-$36/month | Service businesses that also want invoicing, contracts, and proposals in one place | Half a day to a full day |
| No-code app builder | Softr (on top of Airtable) | $49/month + Airtable costs | Consultants with specific logic needs (multiple service types, custom client statuses, internal tools too) | 1-2 days |

Each of these is a legitimate answer. Which one is right depends mostly on whether you already have client management happening somewhere else, or whether the portal is also meant to replace your CRM and invoicing.

## Path 1: The fastest option — turn Notion into a portal

If you're not managing more than a handful of active clients and you already use Notion for your own notes, this is the cheapest and fastest path. It will not look like a polished SaaS product, but it will look organized, and "organized" is most of what clients are actually judging you on.

**How to set it up:**

1. Build one master template page with the sections every client needs: a welcome note, a project timeline or status tracker (a simple database works fine), a "Files" database for deliverables, and an "Invoices" or "Payments" section linking out to your actual invoicing tool.
2. Duplicate that template for each new client rather than rebuilding it from scratch every time.
3. Share the page with the client as a guest, not a member — this keeps your workspace's internal pages private while giving them just their folder.
4. Use a Notion form (built into Plus and above) for client intake so new requests land directly in your database instead of your inbox.
5. Set a custom subdomain if you're on a paid plan, so the link at least reads as yours rather than a generic notion.site address.

**What you're giving up:** Notion was not built to be a client portal — it's a workspace tool you're repurposing. There's no real "client login" with its own password; access is link- and guest-based, which is fine for most solo practices but not something you'd want for anything involving highly sensitive financial or legal documents. You also don't get built-in invoicing, e-signatures, or payment collection — you'll still need a separate tool for those.

## Path 2: A dedicated client-portal platform

If you want the portal to double as your CRM, invoicing, and contract system, a purpose-built platform saves you from duct-taping three tools together. The three most common options for solo consultants, and what they actually cost right now:

**SuiteDash** — Start plan is $19/month ($180/year), and unusually for this price point it includes unlimited client portals and white-labeling from day one, plus a custom-brandable mobile app. The trade-off is the interface: SuiteDash packs in a lot of functionality (LMS, proposals, automation, help desk) and the learning curve is noticeably steeper than Notion or HoneyBook — expect to spend real time in their setup wizard before it feels usable.

<CTA tool="SuiteDash" href="https://suitedash.com" label="See SuiteDash plans" />

**Dubsado** — Starter is $35/month ($335/year) and covers unlimited clients and projects, contracts, invoices, and a client portal with up to 3 user seats. The catch: Dubsado's workflow automation ("Flows") — the feature that actually saves you time on onboarding sequences — is locked behind the $55/month Premier plan. On Starter, you're running the portal manually.

<CTA tool="Dubsado" href="https://www.dubsado.com" label="Compare Dubsado plans" />

**HoneyBook** — Starter is $29/month if billed annually ($348/year), $36/month if paid monthly, and includes a client portal, proposals, contracts, invoicing, and e-signatures out of the box. The limitation is on the team side, not the client side: Starter supports no additional team members at all, and even the mid-tier Essentials plan caps you at two.

<CTA tool="HoneyBook" href="https://www.honeybook.com" label="Explore HoneyBook for solo consultants" />

**Setup, roughly the same across all three:** connect your payment processor (Stripe is standard), build one onboarding workflow template, set your branding (logo, colors, and in SuiteDash's case a custom domain), and create your first client project from the template. Budget half a day if you're reasonably comfortable with software, a full day if this is your first CRM-style tool.

## Path 3: When you need something more custom — Softr

Softr sits in a different category: it's a no-code app builder, not a client-portal product out of the box. You connect it to a database — usually Airtable — and build the portal's pages, login system, and per-client permissions yourself using pre-made blocks. This is the right call if your business logic doesn't fit neatly into what SuiteDash or Dubsado assume (for example, different client types with genuinely different portal views, or you want the same login system powering both a client portal and an internal tool).

Pricing starts at $49/month (Basic) for up to 20 app users, but there's a real catch for solo consultants: branding removal — getting rid of the "Powered by Softr" badge — requires the $139/month Professional plan at minimum. And Softr is a front end, not a database, so you're also paying for Airtable (or another supported backend) separately.

**How to set it up:** build your client and project tables in Airtable first, with a field that ties each record to a specific client's login email. Connect that base to Softr. Use Softr's "User Groups" feature to restrict each client's login to only the records that match their email — this is the step people skip and then wonder why every client can see every other client's data. Build out pages for project status, files, and messages using Softr's block library. Publish and test the permission boundaries yourself, logged in as a fake "client," before sending real invites.

## Which one should you actually pick?

If you're not sure yet whether you'll keep more than a few clients, start with Notion — it's the lowest-commitment option and you can migrate later without having wasted much money. If invoicing and contracts are currently a mess and the portal is really a symptom of a bigger "I need a real CRM" problem, go straight to HoneyBook or Dubsado. If you've already tried a few of these and kept hitting a wall where "the tool just doesn't do what I need," Softr is the escape hatch — but budget real setup time and a second subscription for the database.

## FAQ

**Do I need a custom domain for my client portal?**
No, but it helps with perceived professionalism. SuiteDash supports custom domains on all plans; HoneyBook and Dubsado primarily use their own branded URLs with your logo on top; Notion supports a custom domain on paid plans. None of this is a dealbreaker if you skip it.

**Is Notion actually secure enough for sensitive client files?**
For typical consulting deliverables (reports, slide decks, project trackers), yes — guest access is scoped to the specific page you share. For anything involving signed legal contracts, financial account details, or data under a strict compliance requirement, use a tool built around that, or at minimum keep those documents in a dedicated e-signature/contract tool rather than a shared Notion page.

**How long does this realistically take to set up?**
A bare-bones Notion portal: an afternoon. A SuiteDash, Dubsado, or HoneyBook portal with your first client workflow built: one full day, assuming you're not also redesigning your entire onboarding process at the same time. Softr plus Airtable: plan for a weekend, especially the first time you build the permission logic.

**What if I only have one or two clients right now?**
Don't over-build. A portal pays for itself in time saved once you're juggling enough clients that "where's that file?" emails start eating into your week. Below that, a well-organized shared folder and a consistent naming convention will get you just as far for free.
