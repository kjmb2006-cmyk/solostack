# How to Build a Client Portal Without Hiring a Developer

At some point in every solo consulting practice, the email thread becomes the problem. A client asks for "that deliverable from two weeks ago," you scroll back through forty messages, find a Google Drive link that's since been moved, and resend it with an apology. Multiply that by five active clients and you're spending real hours a week just playing file-retrieval system instead of doing the work you're paid for.

A client portal fixes this: one link, one login, everything in one place — deliverables, invoices, contracts, and a place to leave feedback. The problem is that "client portal" sounds like a software project. It isn't, not anymore. You don't need to hire a developer or learn to code. You need to pick the right tool for how much structure you actually want, and set it up in an afternoon.

This guide walks through three ways to do that — repurposing a tool you probably already pay for, moving into an all-in-one client management platform, or using dedicated white-label portal software — with real pricing and the honest limitations of each.

## What a client portal actually needs to do

Before comparing tools, it's worth being specific about the bar. A working portal for a solo practice needs to let each client log in (or use a private link) and see only their own files and updates, not anyone else's; hold deliverables, contracts, and invoices in one place instead of scattered across email and cloud storage; look enough like your brand that it doesn't feel like you bolted together three free tools; and take less than a weekend to set up, because you don't have a dev team and you don't want this to become a side project.

Everything below is evaluated against that bar, not against what a venture-backed agency with an IT department would build.

## Option 1: Repurpose a tool you already have (Notion)

If you're already running your practice on Notion, the cheapest path to a client portal is building it there instead of adding new software. You create a parent page per client, share it as a guest-accessible page, and nest sub-pages for deliverables, meeting notes, and a simple invoice tracker. Notion's database views can double as a lightweight project tracker the client can see in real time.

Pricing, verified against Notion's current pricing page: the Free plan allows up to 10 guests, which is enough for a handful of early clients. The Plus plan is $10 per member per month (billed monthly; cheaper billed annually) and removes the guest cap along with adding custom forms without Notion branding. The Business plan, at $20 per member per month, adds granular permissions so you can control exactly which rows of a database a guest can see — useful once you have multiple active clients and don't want to rebuild a workspace from scratch for each one.

The honest limitation: guest access in Notion is link- and page-based, not a true multi-tenant client portal with its own login screen. Clients access pages via an invite or link, which is fine for most engagements but not something you'd want for anything involving highly sensitive financial or legal documents — there's no dedicated client-facing dashboard, no built-in e-signature, and no native invoicing. You're assembling a portal out of parts, and it looks like what it is if you don't put real effort into formatting.

## Option 2: All-in-one client management platforms (Dubsado and HoneyBook)

If you're also looking to replace scattered invoicing, contracts, and scheduling — not just file sharing — a dedicated client management platform does more in one place. Dubsado and HoneyBook are the two most established options for service-based solo businesses, and both bundle a client portal into every plan rather than gating it behind a top tier.

**Dubsado**, verified from its current pricing page, runs on annual billing: the Starter plan is $335/year (about $27.92/month) and includes unlimited projects and clients, invoicing, payment plans, and client portals. The Premier plan is $525/year (about $43.75/month) and adds unlimited lead capture forms, bookkeeping integration, and automated workflows. Three users are included free; adding more runs $25–$60/month depending on how many you need.

**HoneyBook** is priced per month and is more transparent about month-to-month vs. annual rates: Starter is $29/month either way, Essentials is $49/month billed annually ($59/month billed monthly) and adds scheduling, automations, and the ability to remove HoneyBook's branding from client-facing pages, and Premium is $109/month billed annually ($129/month billed monthly) and removes the team-member cap entirely.

The trade-off with both: you're moving your invoicing, contracts, and scheduling into a new system, not just adding a portal on top of what you have. That's a real migration, and it only pays off if you intend to actually use the CRM and workflow features — if all you want is a place for clients to grab files, this is more tool than you need. Dubsado's interface also has a steeper learning curve than HoneyBook's; expect to spend a weekend in the workflow builder before it feels natural.

## Option 3: Dedicated white-label portal software (SuiteDash)

If the portal itself — not invoicing or scheduling — is the main thing you need, and you want it to feel fully branded as yours rather than "powered by" a third party, SuiteDash is built specifically around that. Every plan includes unlimited client portals and what SuiteDash calls "extreme white-labeling," meaning your clients see your logo, your domain, and your own branded mobile app rather than SuiteDash's.

Verified current pricing: the START plan is $19/month billed monthly, or $180/year if billed annually, and includes unlimited CRM contacts, unlimited staff, unlimited portals, and 100GB of storage. THRIVE is $49/month billed monthly, or $480/year billed annually, and adds dynamic proposals, a live chat widget for clients, and 500GB of storage. PINNACLE is $99/month billed monthly, or $960/year billed annually, and adds a full automation toolkit, a built-in learning management system, and 2TB of storage.

The catch is that SuiteDash's flexibility comes with real setup overhead — it has more configuration screens than Dubsado or HoneyBook, and most reviewers describe a genuine learning curve before the portal looks the way you want it to. Budget more than an afternoon here; a half-day to a full day is more realistic if you want it polished rather than functional-but-generic.

## FAQ

**Do I need a custom domain for a client portal to look professional?**
It helps but isn't strictly required. HoneyBook and Dubsado portals work fine on their default URLs for most solo practices. SuiteDash is the option built around making the portal and even a companion mobile app feel fully yours, including domain mapping, if that matters for how you position your practice.

**Is a Notion-based portal secure enough for sensitive client documents?**
For typical consulting deliverables — reports, slide decks, meeting notes — yes. For anything involving signed legal documents, financial statements, or data under a strict NDA, a dedicated portal tool with proper access controls and audit trails (SuiteDash, Dubsado, or HoneyBook) is the safer choice, since Notion's guest sharing is based on links and page invites rather than a locked-down client account system.

**Can I switch tools later without losing my client history?**
Generally yes, but plan for manual work, not a one-click export. Files can be downloaded and re-uploaded elsewhere, but workflow automations, proposal templates, and payment history are usually tied to the platform and won't transfer cleanly. It's worth picking based on where you expect to be in a year, not just what's cheapest to start.

**How long does it actually take to set up a basic client portal?**
A Notion-based portal can be functional in an afternoon if you're already comfortable with Notion. HoneyBook and Dubsado both advertise same-day setup for a basic portal, though building out full workflows takes longer. SuiteDash is the most capable but also the one most likely to eat a full day if you want it to look genuinely polished rather than default.
