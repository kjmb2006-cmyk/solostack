# Best Email Newsletter Tools for Consultants Who Want to Build an Audience

LinkedIn can change its algorithm overnight and cut your reach in half. A client can ghost you and take their referral network with them. An email list is the one audience-building asset you actually own — no platform can deplatform it, throttle it, or delete it. For a solo consultant, that makes picking the right newsletter tool a bigger decision than it looks, because switching later means migrating subscribers, rebuilding automations, and hoping your open rates survive the move.

This isn't a "which has the most features" comparison — most of these tools can technically send an email. It's about which one fits how a one-person consulting practice actually operates: not many subscribers yet, not much time to configure software, and a real need for the tool to double as a lightweight CRM for warm leads.

## What a solo consultant actually needs from an email tool

Three things matter more than the rest: how the free or cheap tier scales as your list grows, how much setup effort it takes before you can send your first email, and whether it treats subscribers as people you can tag and segment (useful for consulting, where "past client," "warm lead," and "just curious" are different audiences) rather than just a mailing list.

Deliverability and design polish matter too, but they're roughly comparable across the major players today — the real differentiator for a solo practice is pricing model and how creator-focused versus e-commerce-focused the tool feels.

## Kit (formerly ConvertKit): built for individual creators

Kit's whole design philosophy fits a solo consultant well: subscribers are tagged and segmented as individuals, not dumped into one generic list, and its automation builder (visual, sequence-based) makes it straightforward to build a simple nurture sequence — "download my free guide, get a 3-email sequence, land on a soft pitch for a discovery call" — without needing a marketing background.

Kit's free plan supports up to 10,000 subscribers, which is generous and will cover most solo consulting practices for a long time. The real limitation: pricing scales with subscriber count once you're on a paid tier, and Creator at $33/month gets you unlimited automations and sequences but caps you around 1,000 subscribers on that specific tier's included allowance — meaning your bill grows as your list grows, regardless of whether you're using more features.

## When to consider an alternative instead

**beehiiv** is worth considering if you want your newsletter to function like a public publication rather than just an email — it gives you a hosted website for your newsletter with its own URL, which some consultants use as a lightweight content hub separate from their main site. Scale, the cheapest plan with real features, is $43/month billed annually, and the free plan caps out at 2,500 subscribers — noticeably lower than Kit's free ceiling, so you'll hit a paywall sooner even with a modest list.

<CTA tool="beehiiv" href="https://www.beehiiv.com/?ref=YOUR_AFFILIATE_ID" label="Try beehiiv Free" />

**Mailchimp** makes sense if you're already sending occasional client-facing marketing emails (a promotion, an event invite) and want a single, well-known tool that also handles light newsletter work, rather than running two separate systems. The honest limitation: Mailchimp's free plan caps at 250 contacts, which is small enough that a consultant with even a modest existing network will outgrow it within the first few months, and its interface carries more general-marketing complexity (audience segments, e-commerce tie-ins) than a solo consultant typically needs.

## How to actually start (without overthinking it)

Don't spend a week comparing feature matrices before sending a single email. Pick Kit if you're starting from zero — the free tier is the most generous of the three, and its tagging system means you won't need to migrate later even as your practice grows into needing real segmentation. Write a simple opt-in offer (a checklist, a short guide, a template — something that takes an afternoon, not a month, to create), put a signup form on your site and in your email signature, and send your first email within the first week of setting up the account. A list of 50 engaged former colleagues and past clients is worth more than a perfect design sitting unused. Once your list is live, [automating what happens after someone subscribes](/blog/automate-client-onboarding-make/) is the natural next step.

## FAQ

**Do I need a lead magnet to start collecting subscribers?**

It helps, but it's not required on day one. A simple "get my monthly notes on [your specialty]" signup can work if you already have some visibility (LinkedIn, speaking, referrals). A lead magnet mainly helps convert cold traffic — visitors who don't know you yet — into subscribers, so it matters more once you're actively driving people to your site from outside your existing network.

**How long before an email list actually generates business?**

Most solo consultants see the first inbound inquiry from their list within 3-6 months of consistent sending, assuming they're emailing at least monthly with genuinely useful content rather than only self-promotion. The list compounds — value comes less from any single email and more from staying visible over many months until a subscriber has a need you can solve.

**Can I switch tools later without losing my subscribers?**

Yes, subscriber emails and basic tags export as CSV from any of these three tools and import into the others. What you lose in a switch is automation history and engagement scoring (which subscribers open/click most) — that data doesn't transfer, so plan on rebuilding sequences rather than expecting a clean migration.

**Is it worth paying for a tool before I have any subscribers?**

No. All three have usable free tiers well past zero subscribers — Kit's free plan alone covers up to 10,000. Start free, prove you'll actually send consistently, and only upgrade when you hit a real feature limit (more advanced automation, higher subscriber counts) rather than paying upfront for headroom you may not use.
