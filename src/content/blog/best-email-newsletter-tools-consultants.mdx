Every social platform you post on can change its algorithm, ban your account, or disappear. An email list is the one audience channel a solo consultant actually owns — no platform can take it away or decide to stop showing your posts to the people who asked to hear from you. If you don't have one yet, here's how to think about the tooling.

## What a solo consultant actually needs from an email tool

Not enterprise marketing automation. You need: a simple signup form or landing page, the ability to send a broadcast email to everyone (or a segment), a basic automated sequence (welcome a new subscriber over a few days), and — increasingly useful — a way to sell a digital product or book a call directly from the platform.

## When to consider an alternative instead

- **If you need deep e-commerce integration** (large product catalogs, abandoned cart flows) — a tool built primarily for online stores will serve you better than a creator-focused tool.
- **If you're already deep in a CRM ecosystem** (HubSpot, for instance) and email is a minor part of a larger sales motion — using your CRM's native email tools avoids maintaining two systems.
- **If your list will stay under a few hundred people and you truly only need occasional broadcasts** — a minimal free tool may be all you need, though you'll likely outgrow it once you start segmenting or automating.

For the specific case of a solo consultant building a public reputation — writing about your field, sharing case studies, nurturing leads before they're ready to buy — Kit's creator-first design tends to fit better than general-purpose marketing platforms built for teams.

## How to actually start (without overthinking it)

1. Set up one simple landing page: what the list is about, who it's for, one clear reason to subscribe.
2. Write a 3-email welcome sequence: who you are, what you believe about your field, one useful piece of advice.
3. Commit to a realistic cadence — every two weeks beats weekly-and-then-you-quit-after-a-month.
4. Add your signup link everywhere: email signature, LinkedIn bio, the bottom of every proposal.

The list grows slowly at first. That's normal — the compounding value shows up 12–18 months in, once you have a few hundred engaged subscribers who see you as their go-to person in your field.

## FAQ

**Do I need a lead magnet (free download) to start collecting emails?**
It helps conversion, but it's not required. "Get my weekly notes on [your field]" is a perfectly good reason to subscribe on its own if your writing is genuinely useful.

**How long before an email list produces client leads?**
Plan on 6–12 months of consistent sending before it becomes a reliable source of inbound interest. Treat it as a long-term asset, not a quick channel.
