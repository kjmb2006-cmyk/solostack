Most "best AI tools" lists are written by people who've never run a one-person business and just paid an invoice from savings. This one is organized around the actual jobs a solo consultant needs done — not around which tools have the flashiest demo.

## 1. Automation: Make

For connecting the apps you already use (calendar, CRM, invoicing, email) without hiring an ops person. Its visual canvas handles conditional workflows — "if the client hasn't paid after 7 days, send a reminder" — that simpler tools can't. Free tier covers light use; Core plan starts at $12/month for 10,000 operations.

<CTA tool="Make" href="https://www.make.com/en/register?pc=solostackhq" />

## 2. Project & client management: ClickUp

ClickUp works as a combined project tracker, client-facing task board, and lightweight CRM, which matters when you can't justify paying for three separate tools. The Free Forever plan is genuinely usable for a solo operator; Unlimited starts around $7/user/month billed annually if you outgrow it.

## 3. Docs and knowledge base: Notion

Where ClickUp tracks tasks, Notion is where you keep proposal templates, SOPs, and client-facing docs and wikis. The Free plan covers a single user well; Plus ($10/month) adds custom forms and sites if you want to build a lightweight client portal.

## 4. Meeting notes and follow-ups: Reclaim.ai

Reclaim.ai is built for calendar automation — it defends focus time, auto-schedules recurring tasks around meetings, and syncs habits across calendars, which matters more than it sounds once you're juggling client calls across time zones. Paid plans start around the mid-teens per month with a 25%-recurring-commission affiliate program, which tells you it's aimed squarely at power users, not casual schedulers.

## 5. Writing and editing: Grammarly

Not glamorous, but every proposal, cold email, and LinkedIn post you send is a trust signal. Grammarly's free tier catches the basics; the paid tier adds tone and clarity suggestions that matter when English isn't your first language or you're writing to a client under deadline pressure.

## 6. AI content drafting: Writesonic

Useful for first drafts of case studies, service pages, and social posts — treat it as a drafting assistant you heavily edit, not a publish button. Worth it mainly if you publish content regularly as part of your own marketing.

## 7. Video and audio editing: Descript

If you record client calls, workshops, or make short video content for marketing, Descript's edit-by-editing-the-transcript workflow removes most of the technical barrier to producing decent video. Its affiliate program pays a flat $25 per paid subscriber, which is a useful signal that it converts well for creator-adjacent audiences.

## 8. Email marketing and audience building: Kit

If part of your growth plan is an email list — and for a solo consultant it should be, because it's the one audience channel you fully own — Kit (formerly ConvertKit) is built specifically for creators and independent professionals rather than enterprise marketing teams. Free up to 10,000 subscribers; paid plans add automation sequences.

<CTA tool="Kit" href="https://kit.com/?lmref=YOUR_AFFILIATE_ID" label="Start free with Kit →" />

## 9. Finding leads: Apollo

If outbound is part of how you find clients, Apollo combines a contact database with sequencing so you're not paying for two separate tools. Its affiliate program pays 15–20% recurring, again a sign it's priced for sustained use rather than one-off list purchases.

## How to actually adopt this stack without wasting a month

Don't implement all nine at once. The order that tends to pay back fastest for a new solo practice:

1. **Notion or ClickUp first** — you need one place to track clients and work before anything else matters.
2. **Make second** — automate the one manual task that annoys you most (usually onboarding or invoicing).
3. **Kit third** — start collecting emails now, even before you have a lead magnet; the list compounds.
4. Everything else, add only when a specific bottleneck justifies it.

## FAQ

**Do I need paid plans for all of these on day one?**
No. Notion, ClickUp, Make, and Kit all have usable free tiers for a single-person business. Upgrade tool-by-tool as a specific limit actually blocks you.

**Which one has the best ROI for a brand-new consulting practice?**
Kit, indirectly — an owned email list is the asset that keeps paying off years later, unlike a tool subscription. But Make or ClickUp usually deliver the fastest *time* savings.
